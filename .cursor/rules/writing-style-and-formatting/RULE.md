# Writing style and formatting

Mannered prose substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statement. Instead of "a parameter worth varying," the mannered writer produces "a dial worth turning." Instead of "this point still matters," they write "this point earns its keep." The phrases exist to display the writer, not to convey the idea, and readers can tell. That is why mannered prose irritates: it makes the reader work harder so the writer can perform. It is also imprecise. Metaphors drag in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control. The fix is to say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use it.

Rather than talking about the "price we pay" or "what this buys us", talk in terms of the trade-off, advantage or disadvantage. Avoid referring to what something 'cost' if we aren't actually referring to money - a title like "What the jobs cost" should be "Job walltime summary" or similar. Avoid using the term 'ship', instead say 'deliver' or 'provide', depending on the context. Avoid using the 
term 'load-bearing' unless we are actually referring to the mechanics of a physical structure - maybe you could use 'dependency', 'underpins', 'foundational' or 'critical', depending on the contexted.

Don't use emojis in documentation unless explicitly asked, or particularly appropriate.

DO NOT use the emdash ('—') character in prose - use a normal dash -.
