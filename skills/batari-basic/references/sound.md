Complete reference for batari Basic (bB) sound: the two TIA audio channels, the AUDV/AUDC/AUDF registers, playing and stopping sounds, data-driven sound effects, background music with sdata, and the tone/note charts.

**Covers:** TIA channels 0 and 1 · AUDV0/AUDV1 · AUDC0/AUDC1 · AUDF0/AUDF1 · tone chart (what each AUDC value sounds like) · AUDF note table · playing/stopping a sound · per-frame sound effects without data · data-driven sound effects with data tables and the 255 sentinel · background music with sdata/sread · two sounds at once · muting

## How TIA sound works

- The Atari 2600's sound is generated by the **TIA chip**, which has **two independent audio channels that work identically** — channel 0 and channel 1.
- Each channel has **three registers**: `AUDV0, AUDC0, AUDF0` for channel 0, and `AUDV1, AUDC1, AUDF1` for channel 1.
- There is **no PLAY, SOUND, or BEEP command** in bB (a normal BASIC programmer will look for one and not find it). You make sound by assigning values to these six registers directly.
- Sound is "set it and forget it": set the three registers once and the channel **keeps playing that exact sound continuously** until you change the registers. You do NOT need to re-set them every loop to keep a sound going.
- Sound registers work the **same in every kernel** (standard, multisprite, DPC+). Unlike colors, nothing about audio changes per kernel.
- To make a *changing* sound effect, you change the registers over time — normally once per main-loop pass. `drawscreen` paces the main loop at **60 frames per second** (NTSC), so a duration counted in frames is duration ÷ 60 seconds (a duration of 15 lasts 1/4 second).
- A channel plays one sound at a time; two simultaneous sounds means one on channel 0 and one on channel 1 (see below).

## AUDV0 / AUDV1 — volume

```bb
   AUDV0 = 8
   AUDV1 = 0
```

- Audio **Volume** for channel 0 / channel 1. **Valid values: 0 to 15.**
- `0` = off/mute (silence). `15` = loudest. Useful middle values in the examples: 8 (normal), 9–12 (loud).
- **To stop a sound you MUST set its volume to 0** (`AUDV0 = 0` or `AUDV1 = 0`). A tone with volume > 0 stays on forever until you zero the volume — even if the program moves on to something else.
- **Write-only:** AUDV0/AUDV1 cannot be read. `a = AUDV0` will not work properly. If the register appears anywhere on the right side of an `=` it gets read, so things like `AUDV0 = AUDV0 - 1` are also broken. Use your own variable as a counter instead.

## AUDC0 / AUDC1 — tone (waveform type)

```bb
   AUDC0 = 12
   AUDC1 = 8
```

- Audio **Control** for channel 0 / channel 1 — also known as **Tone, Voice, or Distortion**. Picks the waveform. **Valid values: 0 to 15.**
- **Write-only:** AUDC0/AUDC1 cannot be read (`a = AUDC0` will not work properly).
- Each waveform is basically a repeating stream of 0s and 1s; the pattern length sets the pitch and the pattern complexity sets how "pure" or "noisy" it is. One waveform is "always on" (constant 1s) and sounds silent because the speaker never moves — you can combine it with volume changes to build your own effects.

## AUDF0 / AUDF1 — frequency (pitch)

```bb
   AUDF0 = 10
   AUDF1 = 31
```

- Audio **Frequency** for channel 0 / channel 1 — the pitch/note selector. **Valid values: 0 to 31.**
- **Write-only:** AUDF0/AUDF1 cannot be read (`a = AUDF0` will not work properly).
- **Direction is INVERTED from what you'd assume: a HIGHER AUDF value = a LOWER pitch.** The TIA divides a base frequency by (AUDF + 1): add 1 to the AUDF value and divide the primary frequency by that number. `AUDF0 = 3` divides the primary frequency by 4; `AUDF0 = 7` divides it by 8; `AUDF0 = 31` divides it by 32. So descending melodies count AUDF **up** (31, 26, 23, 20...), and rising pitch means counting AUDF **down**.
- The TIA does not use the standard musical notes (C, D, F#, G...) — each AUDF value lands *near* a note (see the table below), and the exact tuning also depends on which AUDC waveform divides the clock.

## Playing and stopping a sound

Syntax — assign the three registers for the channel, then let the main loop reach `drawscreen`:

```bb
   AUDV0 = 8 : AUDC0 = 12 : AUDF0 = 10
```

- The three assignments can be on one line separated by `:` (colon) statements, or on separate lines. Order doesn't matter; nothing is heard until volume > 0.
- The sound starts and **stays on until you set `AUDV0 = 0`**. Example from the source: `AUDV0 = 10 : AUDC0 = 12 : AUDF0 = 4` produces a tone that stays on until `AUDV0 = 0`.
- To stop: **set the volume to 0** for that channel. Zeroing AUDC also goes silent (values 0/11 are silent) but the standard, documented way is `AUDV = 0`.
- Typically a **frame counter** keeps a sound on for a set number of frames (frames occur 60 times a second): set registers once, count down, then zero the volume.

Short self-contained example — press fire for a 1/4-second beep (it can't retrigger until the beep finishes):

```bb
   dim _Ch0_Sound = c
   dim _Ch0_Duration = d

__Main_Loop
   rem Fire button check (don't restart a sound that's already on)
   if joy0fire then if !_Ch0_Sound then _Ch0_Sound = 1 : _Ch0_Duration = 15

   rem Sound effect check
   if !_Ch0_Sound then goto __Skip_Ch_0
   _Ch0_Duration = _Ch0_Duration - 1
   if !_Ch0_Duration then goto __Clear_Ch_0

   rem Sound effect 001: set tone, volume and frequency (stays on)
   if _Ch0_Sound <> 1 then goto __Skip_Ch_0
   AUDC0 = 4 : AUDV0 = 8 : AUDF0 = 19
   goto __Skip_Ch_0

__Clear_Ch_0
   _Ch0_Sound = 0 : AUDV0 = 0

__Skip_Ch_0
   drawscreen
   goto __Main_Loop
```

This is the "plain sound without data" pattern; the full commented version is `mini_ex_sound_simple.bas` (one sound) and `mini_ex_sound_5_sounds.bas` (five sounds: one per joystick direction plus fire, each with its own `_Ch0_Sound` number and duration) in the skill's `examples/` directory.

## Updating sound per frame

- `drawscreen` limits the loop to 60 passes per second, so "one loop pass = one frame = 1/60 second" — durations in frames (`_Ch0_Duration = 15` → quarter second, `30` → half second).
- Change a register only when you want the sound to *change*: stepping AUDF down a few values over time makes a rising pitch; stepping AUDV down makes a fade-out; stepping AUDC from 12 to 8 mid-effect makes a note turn into noise (explosion tail).
- Registers hold their values between frames, so a one-frame "blip" only needs the registers set once and `AUDV0 = 0` when the counter runs out.
- You can assign variables or temp variables, not just constants: `AUDV0 = _V0`, `AUDF0 = temp6`.

## Data-driven sound effects (data tables)

For anything longer than one flat tone, store the effect as a **data table** and step through it with a counter. This is the standard bB sound-effects pattern (from `ex_sound_using_data_one_sound.bas` and the Sound Effects Template section).

**Table format:** groups of 4 numbers, one group per "frame-step" of the effect:

1. **AUDV** value (volume 0–15)
2. **AUDC** value (tone 0–15)
3. **AUDF** value (frequency 0–31)
4. **duration** in frames (how long this step plays before the next group is fetched)

A volume of 0 in a step is a **rest** (the examples use `0,0,0` rows for silence). The table ends with a **sentinel value of 255** — when the player reads 255 as the first number of a group, the effect is over and the channel is cleared.

**Player variables per channel** (three per channel): a sound number (`_Ch0_Sound`, 0 = off), a duration counter (`_Ch0_Duration`), and a data pointer/counter (`_Ch0_Counter`). To start an effect: `_Ch0_Sound = 1 : _Ch0_Duration = 1 : _Ch0_Counter = 0` (Duration 1 = "fetch the next group on the very next frame").

Complete compact working example (adapted from `ex_sound_using_data_one_sound.bas` — press fire for a drum-style noise burst):

```bb
   dim _Ch0_Sound = q
   dim _Ch0_Duration = r
   dim _Ch0_Counter = s

__Main_Loop
   rem Turn on sound effect 1 if fire pressed and no sound is on
   if _Ch0_Sound then goto __Skip_Fire
   if joy0fire then _Ch0_Sound = 1 : _Ch0_Duration = 1 : _Ch0_Counter = 0
__Skip_Fire

   rem Channel 0 sound effect check
   if !_Ch0_Sound then goto __Skip_Ch_0
   _Ch0_Duration = _Ch0_Duration - 1
   if _Ch0_Duration then goto __Skip_Ch_0

   rem Sound effect 001: step through the data table
   if _Ch0_Sound <> 1 then goto __Skip_Ch_0
   temp4 = _SD_FireB[_Ch0_Counter]        rem volume
   if temp4 = 255 then goto __Clear_Ch_0  rem end of data
   _Ch0_Counter = _Ch0_Counter + 1
   temp5 = _SD_FireB[_Ch0_Counter] : _Ch0_Counter = _Ch0_Counter + 1
   temp6 = _SD_FireB[_Ch0_Counter] : _Ch0_Counter = _Ch0_Counter + 1
   AUDV0 = temp4
   AUDC0 = temp5
   AUDF0 = temp6
   _Ch0_Duration = _SD_FireB[_Ch0_Counter] : _Ch0_Counter = _Ch0_Counter + 1
   goto __Skip_Ch_0

__Clear_Ch_0
   _Ch0_Sound = 0 : AUDV0 = 0

__Skip_Ch_0
   drawscreen
   goto __Main_Loop

   rem Sound data for fire button sound effect.
   rem Rows are: volume, tone, frequency, then duration in frames.
   data _SD_FireB
   15,8,2
   1
   8,8,2
   1
   4,8,2
   1
   0,8,2
   4

   15,8,2
   1
   8,8,2
   1
   4,8,2
   1
   0,8,2
   11

   0,0,0
   8
   255
end
```

Notes on the pattern:

- `_Ch0_Counter` is the index into the table; it advances 4 places per group (volume, tone, frequency, duration) and is reset to 0 when a new effect starts.
- The `255` sentinel is what ends the effect — `if temp4 = 255 then goto __Clear_Ch_0`.
- To add more effects, give each its own `data _SD_Name` table and its own `if _Ch0_Sound <> N then goto __Skip_Ch0_Sound_00N` section; turn one on with `_Ch0_Sound = N : _Ch0_Duration = 1 : _Ch0_Counter = 0`. The Sound Effects Template section suggests `const` names (`const _c_Shoot = 4` etc.) instead of raw numbers so game code reads `_Ch0_Sound = _c_Shoot`.
- The template also shows a random-frequency trick: a reserved AUDF value of 200 in a table row can be replaced at play time with a value picked from a small `data` table of frequencies (`temp5 = (rand/32) : _Freq_Mem = _D_Wall_Freq[temp5]`), so a wall-bounce never sounds identical twice.
- Reference examples in the skill's `examples/` directory:
  - `ex_sound_using_data_one_sound.bas` — the one-effect version of the program above.
  - `ex_sound_using_data_no_bank.bas` — five data-driven effects: four on channel 0 (joystick up/down/left/right) plus one on channel 1 (fire button).
  - `ex_sound_using_data_and_bankswitching.bas` — the same program in a 16k (4-bank) ROM: `set romsize 16k`, sound code in bank 3, data tables in bank 4, reached with `goto __Check_Sound bank3` style cross-bank gotos, plus `set optimization inlinerand` to keep rand fast in bankswitched games.

## Background music while other things happen

Pattern (from `ex_sound_with_bg_music` / `z_bb_ex_sound_with_background.bas` in the skill's `examples/` directory): put game **sound effects on channel 0** and a looping **"music" stream on channel 1**. The music player is a few lines in the main loop; the rest of the loop does gameplay, movement, and collisions exactly as usual — the music just plays underneath.

Music uses **`sdata` (sequential data)** read with **`sread`** instead of regular `data`:

- `sdata _SD_Music01 = u` — defines a sequential table that uses **two adjacent variables** (here `u` and `v`) as its pointer. The table's effective length is limited only by bank size (4k), not the 256 bytes of regular data.
- Unlike regular `data`, the program must actually **run over the `sdata` statement** to (re)initialize the pointer to the start of the table — so it sits under a label (here `__BG_Music_Setup_01`) that the player jumps back to when the stream hits 255.
- `t = sread(_SD_Music01)` returns the next value each call. There is no error at end of data and no restore function — you must put a terminal **255** in the data and check for it, then jump back over the `sdata` statement to loop the tune.
- Same 4-number group format as effects: volume, tone, frequency, duration.
- Start the tune by running over the `sdata` once (e.g. `goto __BG_Music_Setup_01` at game start); the `_Ch1_Duration = 1` line placed right after the `end` makes the first note play on the next frame and is re-applied every time the stream loops.
- In the example, **flipping the left difficulty switch to A mutes the music** by zeroing channel 1's volume.

Channel 1 music player from the example (channel 0 keeps playing effects at the same time):

```bb
__BG_Music_Setup_01
   sdata _SD_Music01 = u
   4,8,2
   1
   8,12,31
   8
   2,8,2
   1
   2,12,31
   8
   4,8,2
   1
   8,12,26
   11
   255
end
   _Ch1_Duration = 1

   rem --- in the main loop: ---
   rem Skips music if left difficulty switch is set to A (mute).
   if !switchleftb then AUDV1 = 0 : goto __Skip_Ch_1
   _Ch1_Duration = _Ch1_Duration - 1
   if _Ch1_Duration then goto __Skip_Ch_1

   temp4 = sread(_SD_Music01)            rem volume
   if temp4 = 255 then goto __BG_Music_Setup_01   rem loop the tune
   temp5 = sread(_SD_Music01)            rem tone
   temp6 = sread(_SD_Music01)            rem frequency
   AUDV1 = temp4
   AUDC1 = temp5
   AUDF1 = temp6
   _Ch1_Duration = sread(_SD_Music01)    rem duration

__Skip_Ch_1
   drawscreen
```

The full program (`z_bb_ex_sound_with_background.bas`) has channel 0 effects for shooting a missile, hitting a wall, hitting the enemy, and player-enemy contact, all while the channel 1 tune loops; `z_bb_ex_sound_bgm_animation.bas` is the same program with an animated sprite, showing that music + animation + gameplay coexist. Gotcha: all data tables (regular or sequential) must live in the **same bank** where they are read — cross-bank reads give wrong data with no error.

## Two sounds at once

- The TIA gives you exactly **two simultaneous sounds: one per channel**. A channel can't layer two sounds; if you start a second effect on channel 0 while one is playing, you must either block the new one (`if _Ch0_Sound then goto __Skip_Fire`) or let it replace it (reset Duration/Counter and go).
- Run two independent copies of the player code: channel 0 variables `_Ch0_Sound/_Ch0_Duration/_Ch0_Counter` with `AUDV0/AUDC0/AUDF0`, channel 1 variables `_Ch1_Sound/_Ch1_Duration/_Ch1_Counter` with `AUDV1/AUDC1/AUDF1`. Both get checked each frame, one after the other, before `drawscreen`.
- Typical split (as in the examples): effects that belong to the player/gameplay on channel 0, and music or a second simultaneous effect on channel 1.

```bb
   rem Channel 0: jump sound (joystick up). Channel 1: shoot sound (fire).
   if joy0up then _Ch0_Sound = 2 : _Ch0_Duration = 1 : _Ch0_Counter = 0
   if joy0fire then _Ch1_Sound = 1 : _Ch1_Duration = 1 : _Ch1_Counter = 0
```

See `ex_sound_using_data_no_bank.bas` for a complete two-channel program (joystick sounds on channel 0, fire button sound on channel 1).

## Common mistakes

- **Looking for a PLAY/SOUND/BEEP command.** bB has none — set `AUDV/AUDC/AUDF` registers directly (three per channel).
- **Forgetting to stop a sound.** A channel plays until you set its volume to 0 (`AUDV0 = 0` / `AUDV1 = 0`). No duration exists in hardware — you must count frames yourself.
- **Reading a sound register.** AUDV0/AUDV1/AUDC0/AUDC1/AUDF0/AUDF1 are write-only. `a = AUDV0` doesn't work, and the register must not appear on the right side of `=` either (no `AUDV0 = AUDV0 - 1`). Keep volume/tone/frequency in your own variables.
- **Assuming higher AUDF = higher pitch.** It's inverted: the TIA divides the base frequency by (AUDF + 1), so bigger AUDF = lower note. AUDF 31 = E3, AUDF 15 = E4 (with AUDC 12).
- **Assuming a duration unit of seconds.** Durations are frames — 60 per second. `AUDV0` held for 60 frames = 1 second.
- **Expecting per-frame re-setting to be needed (or to restart) a sound.** Registers latch: set once and the sound holds. Only write a register when you want the sound to change.
- **Using values outside the ranges.** AUDV 0–15, AUDC 0–15, AUDF 0–31; anything larger wraps and surprises you. Also remember AUDC 0 and 11 are silent no matter what.
- **Reading data across banks.** Every data table (data or sdata) must be read from the bank it lives in; cross-bank reads silently return wrong data.
- **Forgetting the 255 sentinel.** Without the 255 end marker (and the check for it), the player reads past the end of the table into garbage.
- **Putting an `sdata` statement in the normal flow.** Running over `sdata` re-initializes the stream to its start, so it must sit under a label the music player jumps back to — not in the main loop.
- **Trying to play two sounds on one channel.** Only two sounds total, one per channel; guard effect starts with `if _Ch0_Sound then ...` if you don't want a new effect to stomp the playing one.
